Abstract

Modern text-to-speech (TTS) systems systematically misrealize foreign and code-switched words, and the errors are invisible to transcription metrics such as WER and CER. We propose PhARO (Phonetic-Articulatory Reward Optimization): the Phonetic Feature Error Rate (PFER), an articulatory-feature edit distance between the realized IPA transcription and a text-derived target, is used as the reward in GRPO. The target comes from grapheme-to-phoneme conversion, so no reference audio is required, and PhARO steers an existing text-input TTS model without changing its interface or architecture. Trained on CSEAL, our new synthetic English+CJK code-switching dataset, PhARO reduces PFER by 33% over the base model, surpasses proprietary systems, and transfers to held-out benchmarks and monolingual Japanese. Robustness checks (a 27-condition scoring grid, forced-aligned prosodic analysis, a second backbone) and a blind 14-listener study (+1.19 pronunciation MOS at unchanged naturalness) confirm the gains.
